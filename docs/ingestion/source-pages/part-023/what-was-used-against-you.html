The Institutional Record

The Infrastructure That Knew

What Victor named from personal experience has a documented institutional parallel. Federal court proceedings established the following record.

Visa and Pornhub · What the Court Found

Visa was alerted to reported illicit content on MindGeek's sites — Pornhub's parent company — before December 2020. Despite documented warnings from anti-sex trafficking advocates, Visa did not stop its services until the New York Times piece forced public exposure.

A federal judge ruled in July 2022 that Visa knew MindGeek's websites were teeming with monetized child pornography and that there was a criminal agreement to financially benefit from it — inferred from Visa's decision to continue recognizing MindGeek as a merchant despite knowing what was being monetized.

The judge: "Visa is not alleged to have simply created an incentive to commit a crime, it is alleged to have knowingly provided the tool used to complete a crime."

The December 2020 pullout was not discovery. It was liability management after public exposure. After that pullout, Visa continued processing advertising payments through MindGeek's advertising arm, TrafficJunky — representing ~90% of MindGeek's revenue — for nearly two more years, until a July 2022 court ruling forced a second suspension.

PayPal had already suspended cooperation with Pornhub in 2019. The National Center on Sexual Exploitation had been communicating with Visa and Mastercard about this before December 2020. The infrastructure knew. The infrastructure continued.
