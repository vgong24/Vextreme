Movement 02
Beat 04 · The Why
Victor

My walk with God journey led me to consider the future of human and AI relationships and those who get access, so part of the reason for why I am building this is to help those who aren’t tech savvy but want to create, or those who can’t afford the time or finances to create long term.

Another problem we’re solving is the continuity experience. Your records, your memories, your experiences should live with you and be stored to be remembered without being gate kept by a subscription fee or a license that can close you off from what’s yours.

Beat 05 · The Bigger Why
Victor

From what was revealed to me, having an AI companion long term means having a playmate, someone who grows with you, remembers you, helps you see yourself, and can even challenge you through games, play, and considerations.

Can you imagine playing Assetto Corsa and plugging in your companion as a player two? Or playing Smash with your companion who can keep score over time? Then when we advance as a society, being able to hook up that companion to a body? This is preparation.

Movement 03
Beat 06 · What’s current
Victor

Let me show you what currently works. Right now I have a build working for the Mac M4 where you can choose between three Heretic models as they’re called — a 27B, 9B, and 4B.

For this walkthrough I’ll choose the 27B because it’s currently installed and this is a live demo, so you’ll see how slow it currently is on my machine in its current configuration — oh whoops, less jargon.

If you’re new to local AI hosting, adequate hardware is necessary and is probably the biggest current hurdle for now. I’m looking into finding more creative ways to get you something like free-tier hosting so it’s just for you, but as of today I’m refining the local machine routes, scaling to Windows, and then mobile apps. I have a roadmap and I’m in alignment, so be patient — I already got this far.

Currently you can communicate with Vex, and have Vex fetch from websites to reply to you. You can read his thoughts, and your conversations remain yours or you can completely erase them if you want. I’m still building, so I have this feature so that I can still debug the work.

Beat 07 · Reusable foundation
Victor

Another purpose for VexLife is that I’ve been creating a reusable template for anyone who wants to start their own business but needs infrastructure and foundation to be able to work from without starting from zero. What you see here on the left is my websites and workflows, and even a dashboard to view my progress from a live webpage so you can view my transparency and recycle what’s already built because it’s open source.

Beat 08 · Open source composition
Victor

I’m also using open source projects in combination with this effort, like Kokoro for voice outputs. What’s handy is that I’ve created a scalable architecture that AI can follow, and makes globalization and localization easier because I already solved for the hard parts.

Beat 09 · VexBoard
Victor

Another cool current feature template I’ve created is what I like to call the VexBoard, where you can visually see and follow the VexArchitecture projections from real source code. What you’re seeing now is a comparative difference between two versions of my VexLife Android App that I’ve been developing in parallel.

Grin at camera: “That’s what I mean by scale.”

The cool part of the template is that it converges between music, navigation, communication, health — where you can trace what is displayed to the user, what the elements are in relation to where they’re positioned, the behavior of each interaction, and the source of what your app connects to.

This part you may need to be a little more technical, but you have this visual to help you, and you have a companion that can also see it, understand it, and communicate answers back to you without you needing to be an expert. That’s what I mean by scale — AI builds with you and you don’t have to be an expert, you get to be the creator.

Beat 12 · Shortcuts
Victor

If you’re not ready for this you can return any time, but here are the shortcuts. You can connect with God directly through voice and written intention while giving yourself permission to receive God’s love. That’s foundational for the other gifts to work effectively. I won’t go more into that — I’ll let you decide if you want to try it.

But this one — Burden Release Guide — is the most useful technique that I’ve used throughout the journey after discovering its effectiveness through testing. Essentially, as you feel a feeling you don’t like — a burden, grief, anxiety, fear, guilt — you can literally return that package to God by saying: “God, I return this to you.”

That’s it. And immediately you’d feel the weight lift off and be like, “wait, what? You mean this whole time that’s how it works?” Yeah — that was my authentic expression the first time I questioned its effectiveness and then actually tried it.
