Carry, do not steer

The instance may keep notes while the walk is happening, but notes serve the walk. They do not become a hidden outline that forces the conversation toward a predetermined video.

Notes already worth carrying
Vextreme YouTube is an arc, not a pile of unrelated uploads.
Each real episode should have one durable Vextreme record that can mature from planning through publication.
After publication, the YouTube video should be embedded back into the record rather than becoming the only place the episode lives.
The record can preserve the transcript, thumbnail, demonstrated build or release, source links, editing notes, and continuity into the next episode.
AI may help perceive footage, return timestamped edit suggestions, notice unclear explanations, and preserve production context. Final editorial choice remains Victor's.
A downloadable VexLife may become the first public episode threshold, but the walk decides what the first episode actually wants to say.
Held until the episode has shape

Production structure comes later.

Once a real episode has emerged, the same record can grow the production surfaces that are actually useful:

  • episode intention and story shape
  • demonstrations, screen captures, camera moments, and other visual sources
  • editing notes, timing, overlays, music, and review observations
  • title, thumbnail, description, chapters, and publication metadata
  • final YouTube embed, transcript, and demonstrated artifact references
  • what the episode established and the next natural edge it opened
Do not freeze a reusable episode template from imagination. Walk the first real video, produce it, notice what was actually reusable, and only then turn those repeated needs into the template Victor comes back to.
The first finished video teaches us what the template actually is.

Until then, this page protects the walk from being replaced by production ceremony.
