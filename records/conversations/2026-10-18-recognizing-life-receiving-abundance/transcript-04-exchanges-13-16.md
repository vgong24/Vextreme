## What do sand dollars eat?

[Original reply displayed a reference photograph of a living purple Pacific sand dollar.]

Sand dollars primarily consume **tiny particles of food suspended in seawater**, including plankton, microscopic algae, and organic debris.

Their feeding process is fascinating:

1. Tiny spines and microscopic hairlike structures called *cilia* help capture and transport food.
2. Food particles travel along grooves toward the mouth at the center of their underside.
3. Inside, a five-part jaw apparatus called *Aristotle's lantern* helps process food.

That tiny central opening you noticed underneath is therefore especially relevant: **you were looking at the animal's mouth area!**

## How do they move?

[Original reply displayed a photograph of a Pacific sand dollar partially buried in sand underwater.]

Their movement is slow but coordinated.

- **Crawling:** Thousands of tiny spines move in coordinated waves, gradually pushing the animal across the sand.
- **Burrowing:** They angle their bodies and use their spines to dig downward.
- **Standing upright:** In calm water, Pacific sand dollars can stand partially upright, like a coin stuck in the sand, to capture food carried by currents.
- **Lying flat:** When waves become stronger, they flatten against the seabed or bury themselves for stability.

They can even move backward, though forward movement is more common.
