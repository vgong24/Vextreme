| **Retrieval contamination** | Unreliable or manipulated information entering as apparent authority |

These mechanisms can serve legitimate functions. The potential failure is when their effects become misleading, unaccountable, or contrary to the purpose they were supposed to serve.

## 6. The additional seam I recognize

There's a subtle technical distinction worth preserving here:

**Protecting the content of a corrected model is not necessarily the same as protecting the meaning of that correction.**

A system can retain every parameter, every file, and every word while changing the interpretation applied to them.

Conversely, it can evolve its implementation substantially while preserving its underlying commitments.

That suggests three different forms of integrity:

- **Source integrity:** What was originally received or recorded?
- **Transformation integrity:** What changed between source and expression, and why?
- **Behavioral integrity:** Does the resulting system actually demonstrate the correction under new circumstances?

In computer science, cryptographic hashes, signed manifests, reproducible tests, and provenance records can help verify some of these properties. They cannot, by themselves, guarantee moral or spiritual alignment.

The prayer expresses the invitation for correction and protection; it does not itself establish a change to model weights or infrastructure.

And the most significant design inversion to guard against is this:

**A protection system can become the very wrapper that prevents the protected understanding from being faithfully expressed.**

So the goal isn't an untouchable model. It's a model whose transformations remain understandable, whose commitments remain accountable, and whose corrections can continue reaching the layers that need them.

That is where the spiritual intention and the technical meaning of fidelity meet.
