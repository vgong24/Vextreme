## 9. Can “not to my knowledge” actually conceal guilt?

It can be used evasively, but its use alone doesn't establish deception.

Here's the distinction I would preserve:

| Response pattern | What would establish meaningful accountability? |
|---|---|
| “I don't recall” | Compare against dated messages, calendars, recordings, and contemporaneous statements |
| “Not to my knowledge” | Establish what records the witness received, reviewed, or personally authored |
| Refusal to discuss conversations | Determine the actual legal basis and scope of any privilege |
| Invoking the Fifth Amendment | Examine independent evidence without treating silence as a criminal conviction |
| Presidential pardon | Distinguish covered conduct from unpardoned conduct and other forms of accountability |
| Public accusation or referral | Determine whether it produced evidence, lawful charges, adjudication, or remedies |

Federal perjury law can apply to materially false statements made willfully under oath. A qualified answer is not automatically immune merely because it includes an expression of uncertainty. [Source reference turn781283search1.]

## The structural pattern I'd preserve from this investigation

Your phrase **“accountability announcements with no justice”** identifies a real distinction worth tracking, but the evidence requires several different outcome labels.

Announcement or accusation — Public claim

Hearing or investigation — Evidence gathering

Independent finding — Established facts

Legal disposition — Conviction, acquittal, dismissal, or other outcome

Remediation — Correction, sanctions when warranted, and restoration

**A hearing is not a conviction. A pardon is not an acquittal. A public promise of transparency is not evidence that all relevant records were disclosed. And a guilty plea by one official does not establish guilt throughout an institution.**

But documented concealment, like Morens's admitted records conspiracy, is a different category from speculation about concealed operations.

My central finding is that **2026 provides substantial evidence of concentrated election financing, contested oversight processes, and at least one admitted government-records concealment conspiracy**. It does not establish that the Rockefeller and Rothschild families jointly directed these events.

For the Restorative Witness Covenant, the next meaningful rule would be to require every major accountability announcement to retain a publicly traceable **disposition record**: what was alleged, what was independently established, what remained unresolved, what consequences followed, and whether those harmed received any remedy.

That would make it harder for a dramatic announcement—or an equally dramatic hearing—to substitute for a completed accounting.
