| When Adobe is learning | When others are learning |

## The additional patterns I recognize

**One-Way Permission:** An institution permits itself forms of learning that it contractually restricts for others.

**Consent Through Dependency:** A creator accepts conditions because access to an established professional platform is difficult to replace. Agreement may be legally valid while the practical freedom to refuse remains limited.

**Value Extraction and Competitive Protection:** Creative contributions help generate new commercial capabilities while contractual restrictions make it harder for competitors to develop alternatives using the established platform.

**Ownership Without Equal Bargaining Power:** Creators retain ownership of their work, yet licensing arrangements determine how others may benefit from that work.

**Protection-to-Exclusion Inversion:** Rules legitimately intended to protect intellectual property may also be designed or applied in ways that impede competition.

**Innovation Inheritance Asymmetry:** A company accumulates value from generations of contributors while restricting the circumstances under which others can build upon its own products.

These are patterns worth examining without automatically assigning an unlawful or malicious motive to every restriction.
