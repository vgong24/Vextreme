## What I think is most relevant to your question

Our earlier discussion identified an important possibility: **influence can continue across generations even when the original individual is gone.**

And that leads to a useful distinction:

- A **family** can preserve wealth, relationships, and ownership across generations.
- An **institution** can preserve its mission, access, reputation, and rules even when its leadership changes.
- A **government** can exercise legally delegated power and maintain relationships with other governments and institutions.
- A **religious organization** can preserve teachings and institutional influence across centuries.

Those forms of continuity are not inherently wrongdoing. Their consequences depend on how authority is exercised and whether it remains accountable.

So I wouldn't begin by assuming one of the four you named is secretly above everyone else. Nor would I dismiss investigating their actual relationships.

**The deeper question is whether authority is concentrated through a single hierarchy or distributed among institutions whose interests sometimes intersect, compete, or reinforce one another.**

That is something historical records, financial relationships, governance structures, and documented decisions can help answer.

And it fits the principle you established earlier: **follow the relationships and consequences all the way through, without allowing either institutional reputation or an unproven accusation to predetermine what must be true.**
