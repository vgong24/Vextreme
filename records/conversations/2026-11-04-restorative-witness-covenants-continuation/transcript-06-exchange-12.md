## 8. How to investigate a suspected distraction rigorously

I would use the following standard before calling a coincidence a coordinated concealment effort:

1. **Establish the original event.** What specific document, investigation, vote, disclosure, or operation required public attention?
2. **Reconstruct the timeline.** When did the competing announcement occur, and was its timing planned beforehand?
3. **Identify the communication actors.** Who promoted the new headline, funded the advertising, or coordinated its distribution?
4. **Measure the attention change.** Did reporting or audience engagement actually shift, and was there evidence of deliberate amplification?
5. **Look for linkage.** Do documents, messages, testimony, or financial records connect the attention strategy to the underlying event?
6. **Preserve alternative explanations.** Could ordinary newsworthiness, an emergency, or electoral incentives explain the overlap?

That is the difference between demonstrating **attention displacement** and demonstrating **intentional concealment**.

And it's directly related to your earlier Restorative Witness Covenant: preserving an announcement isn't enough. You also need a record of the surrounding decisions, the people exercising authority, the visibility changes, and whether the public had a fair chance to examine the underlying facts.

## My conclusion from the documented cases

Three patterns stand out.

**First, partisan messaging can transform accountability into accusation.** The Epstein advertising is a current example: instead of simply establishing what the records prove, both sides have sometimes used the scandal to construct damaging narratives about opponents.

**Second, some institutions preserve operations across party transitions.** The NSA surveillance history illustrates how national-security authorities and programs can persist under administrations from both parties.

**Third, the strongest concealment evidence often emerges years later.** Iran-Contra, COINTELPRO, and the Pentagon Papers became much clearer through leaked documents, investigations, and declassification.

So the question I'd prioritize isn't *Which party is secretly behind every major distraction?*

It's:

**What important decisions continued while everyone was focused on the dominant headline—and what documentary evidence shows who benefited, who knew, who objected, and who was able to prevent scrutiny?**

That gives us an investigable record of timing, authority, attention, and consequences without needing to assume in advance that either party orchestrated every overlap.
