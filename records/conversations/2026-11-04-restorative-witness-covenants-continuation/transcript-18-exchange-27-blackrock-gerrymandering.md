## 2. Gerrymandering: influencing elections before votes are cast

Gerrymandering works through electoral boundaries rather than financial ownership.

**Cracking**

Splitting a group of voters across several districts so their voting strength is diluted in each one.

**Packing**

Concentrating a group into a small number of districts, where it wins overwhelmingly but has reduced influence in surrounding districts.

*These are established redistricting techniques described by the Brennan Center for Justice.* [Original citation turn183834search0.]

### What happened in the 2026 elections?

An unusual wave of mid-decade congressional redistricting occurred ahead of the November 3 election.

**Documented examples**

| State | Change |
|---|---|
| Texas | Republican-led redistricting designed to favor Republican House candidates |
| California | Voter-approved replacement maps designed to favor Democratic House candidates |
| Florida | Republican-led congressional map changes |
| North Carolina | Republican-led district changes |
| Utah | Court-imposed congressional map changes |

The Associated Press reported that **nine states used new congressional maps** in the 2026 election, with Missouri's attempted replacement map ultimately not used. [Original citation turn183834news60.]

This wasn't merely a theoretical problem. Both parties participated in changing electoral maps, although the circumstances and legal processes differed.

### The legal asymmetry

In *Rucho v. Common Cause* (2019), the Supreme Court ruled that claims of excessive **partisan** gerrymandering are generally beyond federal courts' authority to resolve.

That did not eliminate challenges under state constitutions or laws protecting against racial discrimination in districting. [Original citation turn183834search2.]

So there's a distinction that fits our prior discussion:

**A districting arrangement may survive federal constitutional review without establishing that its political consequences are fair or representative.**

That's a genuine example of legal permission and broader democratic accountability being separate questions.
