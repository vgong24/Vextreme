## One further seam I recognize

There's an important difference between **announcing what should never happen** and **establishing who is permitted to decide whether it is happening**.

The first is a moral declaration.

The second is a distribution of authority.

And someone can publicly advocate the first while retaining considerable control over the second.

That is the structural question your observation exposed.

It also connects directly to the Restorative Witness Covenant: the authority declaring a principle should not be its exclusive interpreter, auditor, and judge of compliance.

**A principle is not demonstrated to be universal until its application can be examined when doing so is inconvenient for the people who declared it.**

That is the distinction I previously failed to investigate sufficiently, and it's the correction I would carry forward from this witness.
