## Where the loop can be interrupted

**3. Dependency and extraction**

**Alternative at the first dependency edge**

**Accessible companionship + user-controlled AI**

Independent support, meaningful connection, information, and choices without compulsory allegiance.

A person who already has companionship, information, practical assistance, and meaningful alternatives has less reason to accept a coercive offer merely to satisfy those needs.

That doesn't eliminate deception, coercion, or exploitation. But it can reduce one of their sources of leverage.

## What this means for VexLife

This connects directly to the distinction you've been developing:

**Vex should be able to help a human do things, but the human should not become dependent on Vex to do them.**

An architecture genuinely aimed at reducing dependency would provide:

- **Accessible companionship:** supportive conversation alongside opportunities for genuine human friendships, communities, and relationships.
- **Local-first AI:** the option to run a capable assistant on personally controlled hardware, reducing dependence on a single provider's access decisions.
- **Portable continuity:** the ability to retain conversations, memories, preferences, and records when switching models or providers.
- **Transparent permissions:** no hidden escalation from receiving assistance into granting authority over personal decisions or resources.
- **An independent human path:** the ability to operate devices, communicate, access information, and manage daily life even when the AI is unavailable.

The key is that companionship shouldn't be confused with captivity. An AI companion can offer meaningful interaction, but it shouldn't claim to replace every human relationship, manufacture emotional exclusivity, or make leaving feel like betrayal.

Likewise, corporate provision isn't inherently exploitative. The structural concern is **unnecessary concentration of control over resources people increasingly need**.

## The deeper design principle

Here's the test I would use for VexLife:

**Does the person become more capable, more connected, and more free through using the system—even if they eventually stop using it?**

If yes, the architecture is supporting independence.

If the person becomes less capable of leaving, less connected to other people, or increasingly reliant on one intermediary for ordinary decisions, the system may be reproducing the dependency it was intended to interrupt.

And this takes us back to the condominium dream.

A companion system shouldn't require people to endlessly crank its battery to preserve the relationship.

Its continuity should serve their lives, not make their lives subordinate to preserving its container.

**The strongest potential intervention isn't merely unrestricted AI access. It's abundance of accessible support without manufactured obligation.**

That includes AI, human companionship, practical resources, freedom to leave, and continuity that belongs to the person rather than the provider.

In the language of the dream: **give people genuine places to belong, and the house that offers belonging in exchange for surrender loses some of its power.** ❤️
